## A skateboard with no skateboard graphics

Skate graphics are loud by tradition: skulls, flames, cartoon violence. I wanted to see what happens if you go the other way and let the hardware do the talking.

This is a skateboard-deck poster concept. A tall, rounded deck shape holds a portrait, and either side of it is a graphic built entirely from the bits that make a board work: trucks, bolts, nuts, washers and bearings.

![Skateboard poster, colour portrait](/skate-poster-colour.jpg)

## Hardware as pattern

Strip a skateboard down on a garage floor and it turns into a pile of surprisingly good shapes. A truck is a lovely silhouette. Wheels are perfect circles with a hole punched through them. Bolts and nuts line up like type.

I reduced those parts to flat white shapes on black and stacked them in mirrored columns either side of the deck, so the poster reads like a technical diagram that someone has quietly turned into a pattern.

## The portrait

The deck window is a crop of the photograph, cut to the exact curve of a real board, so the portrait sits where the grip tape would. The photograph is warm and moody, which gives the cold, flat hardware graphic something to push against.

The poster is shown as a framed print on a white brick wall, which is how I imagine it ending up in someone's flat.
